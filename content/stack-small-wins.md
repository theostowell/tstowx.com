I think a lot.

And, amongst all the thinking, I land on one recurring life principle, which you might call my axiom:

**_Stack small wins._**

What does this mean?

Well, I can explain on a practical level, and on a philosophical one.

Let’s start with the former, which is more obvious…

It’s harking to momentum, stacking bricks upwards, rather than tearing them down, in the form of 'good' decisions.

(*I'm going to stop 'quote-marking' things here as a form of hedging, and assume you're not trying to pick holes in what I'm saying here, thanks*)

The outcomes of small battles and, hopefully, small successes are what make up the fabric of your life.

On the simplest level, for example, you choose to put the phone down instead of opening Instagram, or you choose the home-made flapjack, instead of the processed chocolate biscuit when stood in front of the fridge.

The more better decisions you make, the more likely your life's going to come out the way you want it to when it comes to the more monumental things (you can't control these in the moment per se., which I'll discuss in the next section).

You can't suddenly decide to create a great piece of art, unless gripped by a moment of divine inspiration. A more reliable way of creating such work is to build your way there through many small efforts, essays, articles, reels and carousels (in this age), instead of sitting there and waiting for the powers who (may or may not) be to strike you with feverish spurts of talent.

*(It's happened to me perhaps once in my life, and was heavily dependent upon environment, connection and conversation, and doesn't read as great as it felt writing it, when I look back now, so I was likely blinded in the moment)*

A cat can't suddenly become a dog, and it's pretty similar with the character and skills we've had our whole life, unless you stack small wins, leading to changes that are undetectable in the moment, but make a big difference once compounded over time.

And then the fantastic result appears. Yes, it seems overnight, but it's like what they taught us in chemistry - once the particles have enough energy (through heat, or some other medium), the activation point is reached, and the reaction happens, but we must remember that the substance had to get warmer by 1 degree after another in the first place. 

Those are the equivalent to our small wins, before the large win or fork in the roads, the outcome of which isn't particularly controllable in the moment anyway...

This leads us to our second main point, about the idea of free-will...

---

Free will doesn’t exist in the way it’s generally perceived. 

Yes, you can pick what you do in your life, at least to an extent, but most of the big things that end up happening to you (it's in the word choice 'end up happening'), you likely can't change or avoid.

And it's in this understanding that I choose to believe in free will on a micro-scale, but pre-determinism in the maxi-scale.

Willpower aside (as this isn't a particularly tangible concept anyway), it's a pretty binary choice whether you're going to do a small bad thing or a small good thing.

And so you just need to engage your mind for a moment and choose the good thing. This is where The Flinch comes in (I read a book a while ago about how to 'flinch' forward into choosing the right thing, and many friends have said it's the one most influential idea that I've suggested to them).
