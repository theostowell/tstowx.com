I’ve been shooting photos pretty much since I was old enough to listen to my mother’s instructions on how to use a point-and-shoot.

Over time, this developed into a passion, saving birthday and Christmas money so that I could bid for used bodies and lenses on eBay, and later purchase upgrades through MPB.com, a second-hand marketplace for camera equipment in the UK.

After years of watching Attenborough documentaries and visiting WWT Slimbridge in Gloucestershire, I decided that I wanted to film wildlife for a living.

Hence, after discovering that most of the people in the field studied zoology, I shot off to Sheffield to do the same.

And that’s where the trail went somewhat quiet.

Here’s the truth - I started shooting photos freelance for a client or two, but at the same time, I studied and started an internet business that’s actually earning revenue, so I’ve been in a limbo of which option to pursue.

In an ideal world, I’d be able to do both. The internet stuff so that I’ve got income if I want to travel to a new place, or spend some time abroad (as it would take time to acquire photo clients there anyway), and the photo work for when it’s nice out in the summer and I want to meet new people.

That’s what happened throughout the weekend just passed. I spent it in London, sleeping on the floor of a good friend’s apartment, and on Saturday shot an event for friends I met in the city over a year ago.

Luckily, since coming back from Asia, I’ve been blessed with good weather in the UK, and so spending time outside and meeting new people is the best way to spend time.

But I’d want the option to some days be outside shooting, and others where I can just stay inside and build traction on the internet instead.

A long time before this, I said the end form of what all of this online/creative work looks like is where people trust your personal brand and capabilities enough for you to simply be able to create whatever you want (for me this would be writing as well as photos), and make a living from this, Tim Ferriss-style.

And that’s where I’d love to see myself. So even at the same time as talking about prioritising and keeping things simple, I’m trying to cover all the bases, and earn a living, and right now this leans me a little further away from the freelance photography work.

Fear not, though. The plan’s coming together.
